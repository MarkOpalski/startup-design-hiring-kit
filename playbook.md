# Startup Design Hiring Playbook

This playbook is a practical guide for startup founders and product leaders to make better design hires—especially when hiring your first designer.

It’s based on real-world experience, community patterns, and common missteps made at early startup stages.

---

## 1. Diagnose Your Needs

Before hiring, ask:

- What stage are we at? (Pre-seed, Seed, Series A?)
- Do we need raw output (UI, UX, flows) or strategic design leadership?
- Do we have engineering support? PM support?
- Do we need a “designer who ships” or a “system-builder”?

---

## 2. Common Founder Mistakes

- Hiring based on brand name (FAANG) instead of skill fit
- Hiring too senior or too junior for the current needs
- Expecting unicorns who do brand, UX, UI, and front-end
- Valuing aesthetics over usability or systems thinking
- Rushing to hire without alignment on what success looks like

---

## 3. Understand Design Role Types

| Role Type | Best For | Risks |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Founding Designer (IC) | MVPs, fast iteration, generalist work | Can get overwhelmed without structure |
| Fractional Design Lead | Strategic hiring, systems, mentoring ICs | Not full-time, limited availability |
| Design Director | Scaling team, design org leadership | Overkill for <10-person teams |
| Design Advisor | Hiring help, critique, feedback loops | Not a shipping resource |

See [`/roles`](./roles) for more.

---

## 4. Hiring Process

- Define what outcomes you want in the next 6 months
- Write a job description (see `/templates`)
- Source candidates via your network, design communities, and advisors
- Use a paid trial project for top candidates (see `/interview/trial-project-guide.md`)
- Ask thoughtful interview questions (see `/interview/questions.md`)
- Make an offer with clear expectations and equity philosophy

---

## 5. Build Design Culture Early

- Treat design as a partner, not a service
- Integrate design into sprint planning and product thinking
- Run async design reviews with Loom or Figma comments
- Prioritize user research—even in small batches
- Encourage feedback loops with PMs and engineers
